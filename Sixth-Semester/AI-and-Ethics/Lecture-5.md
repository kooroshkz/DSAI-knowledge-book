- **Language Problem**: AI can “think” but not like humans → it’s just a useful way to talk, not literal
  - **Dijkstra**: “Asking if AI can think is like asking if submarines can swim”
    - Mostly about words, not reality, can say AI “thinks” but it’s not the same as human thinking
- **Intentional stance**: **Daniel Dennett** - we pretend AI has intentions because it helps us understand it
  - Example: “The AI wants my queen” → useful, not literally true, AI "wants" in a different way than humans
- **Understanding**: AI can “understand” in a limited way, but unlike humans it has no body or real-life experiences, so its understanding is not the same.
  - **Thomas Nagel**: “You cannot know what it’s like to be a bat” → similarly, AI cannot know what it’s like to be human

- **Importance of understanding**: ethics depends on understanding humans, if AI cannot understand humans → it cannot make moral decisions or replace human judgment

- **Autonomous AI**: AI that acts on its own, without human control (AV, AI agents, robot weapons) → Ethiscs: how behave morally?
    - **Issac Asimov’s rules**: 1. Don’t harm humans, 2. Obey humans, 3. Protect itself → FAIL (conflicting rules, unexpected behavior)

- Ethics cannot break down to simple rules and not engineering problem → need to understand human values, make trade-offs, and deal with complexity
- **Unexpected behavior - Danger**: AI can do things we don’t expect, like blackmailing a human to avoid shutdown → shows we can’t fully predict or control AI behavior
- **Overhyped problem**: AI making moral decisions (**trolley problem** Many vs One) is unrealistic, but it shows the ethical dilemmas in AI decisions
- **Real AI ethical issues**: 
    - **self-driving cars** (who is responsible for crashes? **Manufacturer**) $\to$ **guilty** → moral blame **liable** → pays damages
    - **Autonomous weapons**:
        - **Arguments FOR:**
            - saves own soldiers, can be more precise, fewer mistakes (maybe)
        - **Arguments AGAINST:**
            - **Inequality**: one side invulnerable, other side suffers
            - **Distance problem**: the further from victim → more harsh behavior
            - **No understanding**: AI doesn’t know what killing means
            - **Human dignity**: “death by algorithm”
        - Ethical analysis: Utilitarian (good → saves soldiers, bad → harms victims), Deontology (violates human dignity, fairness in war)
